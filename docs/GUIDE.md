# Using Dictate & Delegate

## Set up

Install the APK (Android 14+) and open Dictate & Delegate. You need an [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai) account with a few dollars of credits: Dictate & Delegate calls the models with your own key and has no server of its own. Setup also shows you how Dictate & Delegate works:

1. **Your OpenRouter key.** Create one at [openrouter.ai/keys](https://openrouter.ai/keys), paste it, tap **Check and save**. Dictate & Delegate checks it with OpenRouter and keeps it encrypted on the phone.
2. **Microphone and privacy.** Allow the microphone and read what leaves your phone.
3. **Transcript first, then you decide.** What the floating panel offers (below).
4. **Start and insert.** Add the Dictate & Delegate tile to Quick Settings in one tap, or choose to start by holding a volume key. Allow the floating panel (display over other apps).
5. **Accessibility.** Lets **Insert** put text at the cursor and lets Dictate & Delegate notice the volume key. Android opens its Accessibility settings; tap Dictate & Delegate and turn it on. If Android says the setting is restricted: App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings, then try again. Without it, Insert copies instead.
6. **Try it once.** Start, say a sentence, stop, tap the practice box, then **Insert** on the panel.
7. **Connect bb, Chief of staff, Notifications** (optional, **Later** skips them): see [Your chief of staff in bb](#your-chief-of-staff-in-bb). Without bb, Dictate & Delegate is a dictation app.

Settings → **Models & providers** → **OpenRouter key** replaces or removes the key. Without one, Dictate & Delegate doesn't record.

## Record

Start with the Dictate & Delegate tile in Quick Settings, by holding the volume key you chose, or with **Start dictating** in Dictate & Delegate. Speak, switch apps, read, continue. Silence never stops recording. Stop with ■ on the floating panel, the tile, or by holding (or pressing) the volume key again.

**Volume key:** Settings → **Start** picks off, volume down or volume up, and how long to hold (0.5 s by default). A short press still changes the volume. It works while the screen is on and unlocked, not during calls.

Dictate & Delegate needs no language setting: the speech model recognises the language, also when you switch languages in one note. Settings → **Models & providers** → **Transcription** (or **Speech model** on Home) picks the model (all of them take your words as spelling hints); the default is MAI-Transcribe 2.

## The floating panel

The panel's buttons are icons, named for TalkBack; a long press shows the name. While you record, one compact row shows the timer with ■ (stop), **✨ Refine**, **🧑‍💼 Chief**, ✕ (cancel) and 📎. Tapping Refine or Chief now means "do this when the transcript is ready" (✓); tap again to undo. ✕ throws the recording away: nothing is transcribed or sent, so it costs nothing.

After you stop, your words are transcribed and the panel offers the following. Dictate & Delegate never puts anything on your clipboard by itself, so whatever you copied stays there:

- **📥 Insert:** puts the transcript at the cursor of the field you're typing in, when you tap. You can move the cursor or switch fields first. Insert sets the field's text directly, without the clipboard; only an app that accepts nothing but a paste gets it through the clipboard, and the panel says so. With no field, or without the Accessibility permission, the text is copied instead and the panel says "Copied instead, paste with a long press".
- **✨ Refine:** one call to the text model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 unless you pick another) makes the text you need: it works out what the text is for (message, email, note, reply…) and improves it towards that, follows what you asked ("more formal", "turn it into bullet points", "translate to German"), fills in a template you named, and keeps your language and the facts. Then the button becomes ✨📥, **Insert refined**: if the transcript you inserted is still unchanged in the field, it's replaced in place; otherwise the refined text goes at the cursor.
- **🧑‍💼 Chief:** hands what you said to your chief of staff in bb (below). Then the button becomes 🧑‍💼↗, **Open chat ↗**. Whatever goes to the chief goes as said: a Refine that Jev started is dropped.
- **✕:** closes the panel. The note stays in Dictate & Delegate.

The panel never takes focus, so the cursor stays in your app; drag it anywhere. It hides after a minute without a tap (five while Refine runs or the chief's text is awaited).

**What starts by itself.** Jev, a fast decision model (about 0.3 s and $0.00003 per note), reads the transcript:

- When you ask for a text or name a template, **Refine** starts by itself.
- When your chief's name comes up (Settings → **Chief of staff** → **Your chief's name**, for example "Ada"; it saves as you type and says ✓ Saved), Jev decides whether the dictation is meant for the chief: "Ada, …", "this is for Ada", "I want to talk to Ada about…", "send this to Ada". Then it goes to the chief by itself, and isn't refined. A name that only comes up in what you say ("say hi to Ada from me") stays with you. If Jev isn't sure, or fails, 🧑‍💼 is highlighted instead. The name also matches common mishearings (Henry: Henri, Henrie, Henry's).

Settings → **Models & providers** → **Decisions** turns both off (**Refine automatically**, **Send to chief when addressed**); Settings → **Dictation** → **Refine instructions** edits what Refine is told. If Jev fails, nothing starts by itself; the buttons still work. If Refine fails, the panel and the note say why and offer to try again.

## Your chief of staff in bb

[bb](https://getbb.app) runs coding agents (threads) on your own computer. Dictate & Delegate can hand a task you speak to one bb thread, your **chief of staff**, which handles it or starts child threads for bigger work. When it's done, or when it needs you, it notifies you through [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh); tapping the notification opens the thread in the bb web app, where the answer (and any text to paste) is.

Tap **🧑‍💼 Chief** on the panel, or say it's for your chief by name (above). The dictation as said, plus pasted text from 📎 and a short request ID, goes to the chief; images and PDFs from 📎 go along as attachments, images first, so the chief can look at them. Activity shows how many files went along. If bb doesn't take the upload directly (bb Connect refuses it), they go as base64 text in parts that the chief joins and decodes first; Activity then says "(as base64)" and which HTTP status bb refused the upload with. A file over 3 MB can't go that way; the chief is told it was attached, and the dictation always goes. The panel then offers **Open chat ↗**, and the note shows **Sent to chief of staff ↗** and **What you said**. If bb can't be reached, the note keeps what you said, says why, and offers **Send again**.

**Text back from the chief.** When you ask for something to paste ("draft a reply to Sarah and send me the text"), the chief posts it to your ntfy topic, tagged with the request ID. While the panel is open, Dictate & Delegate reads the topic and shows **Insert from chief**. Otherwise the ntfy notification has a **Copy** button, which hands the request ID to Dictate & Delegate; Dictate & Delegate fetches the text from your topic and copies it (this needs the Accessibility service on, since that's what receives it). The note keeps the chief's text.

Set it up in setup or Settings → **bb**:

1. **Connection.** bb's MCP server (plugin `bb--mcp`) must be on.
   - **bb Connect:** enter your handle (`<handle>.getbb.app`) and tap **Sign in with bb**. Your browser opens bb's account page; approve Dictate & Delegate there and you come back signed in. **Sign out** revokes the sign-in.
   - **Custom address:** an MCP address and an optional bearer token, for example bb's local address `http://127.0.0.1:38886/api/v1/plugins/bb--mcp/http/mcp` reached through your tailnet (Tailscale Serve, HTTPS) or `adb reverse tcp:38886 tcp:38886`. Plain HTTP is allowed only to 127.0.0.1. Also enter the bb web address used for thread links (`https://<handle>.getbb.app`).
   - **Test connection** / **Save and test** checks that bb answers and offers its `run` tool.
2. **Chief of staff.** The chief's instructions are two bb user skills: **chief-of-staff** (how it works for you: small things itself, bigger work in parallel child threads it briefs with context, intent and what done looks like; asks you when a decision is yours) and **dictate-and-delegate** (dictations end with a request ID; one ntfy notification when a request is done or needs you; text to paste as an ntfy message tagged with the request ID, with a Copy button). Dictate & Delegate fills in your name, your ntfy topic and server and the bb links, and installs them in `~/.bb/skills` through a short bb thread, which it archives afterwards. **Create chief of staff** installs them if needed, then starts a thread called "Chief of staff" in Personal (bb's "no project") that only gets "You're my chief of staff. Use the chief-of-staff and dictate-and-delegate skills." Or paste the link or id of a thread you already have; it gets the same line (a chief from before the skills gets it once, after they're installed). Settings → **Connection** shows whether bb has the current skills, asks for **Your name** (optional; without it the skills say "the user"), and **Check the skills in bb** checks again. When the name, the topic, the ntfy server, the bb links or the app version change, Dictate & Delegate updates the skills by itself.
3. **Notifications.** Dictate & Delegate makes a long random ntfy topic once (anyone who knows it can read the notifications). **Open in ntfy** subscribes in the ntfy app (Google Play or F-Droid), **Send test** posts a test message from the phone. Self-hosting ntfy? Enter your server; the chief is told.

Dictate & Delegate itself sends no notifications: the chief runs `curl` to ntfy on your computer.

## Hands-free with your chief

For driving or cooking, in English for now. Start it from Home → **🎧 Hands-free with your chief** → **Start hands-free**, or while dictating with **📞** on the floating panel: what you've said so far becomes the start of your first turn, and it keeps listening. It needs a chief of staff in bb. Talk as you think, with pauses as long as you like; nothing is sent until you say it.

- **What it's doing, and why:** the hands-free screen shows one live line for right now: **Listening**, **Hearing you**, **Checking whether you're done…** (with the seconds when it's slow), **Listening · not sent yet**, **Sending…**, **Sent ✓**, **Waiting for the chief**, **Reply waiting · plays when your turn is over**, **Reply coming…**, **Playing the reply** or **Mic paused after the reply**, and **Voice failed: …** with the cause (HTTP status and what the voice model said, or how long it didn't answer) when a voice model fails. Below it, **What it decided, and why** lists the latest decisions in plain words, such as "Send ignored: just sent (0.4 s ago)", "Didn't count as speech: too short (260 ms, 400 ms needed)", "Reply waits: you're talking", "Not sent: you seem mid-sentence (4% done)" or "Mic paused 0.8 s after the reply (echo)". Your words ("You: …") are updated at each pause, when they're written down. The call bar floating over other apps shows the same state in short (a dot with **Listening**, **Hearing you**, **Not sent yet**, **Sent ✓**, **Working…**, **Reply waiting**, **Reading**…), a send button (paper plane) whenever you can send, and the red hang-up; the notification shows the state and the latest reason. Switched to with 📞 the bar appears where the dictation panel was; drag it anywhere, and tap the state for the full screen, with your words ("You: …") or the reply ("Chief: …") and what the call has cost so far.
- **Sounds:** quiet, short sounds mark the key moments: a soft tick when it starts hearing you, two rising notes when your message is sent, two falling notes when a reply is coming, and two low notes when something failed. They never count as your speech. Turn them off in Settings → Hands-free.
- **Live transcription (the quickest):** with your own key for AssemblyAI or ElevenLabs (Settings → Hands-free → Live transcription model), what you say is written down while you speak, and a send word at the end ("over"; your send words) sends your turn at once, without waiting for a pause: Jev is asked right away whether you're really done, and its answer comes in the moment you're quiet after the word, so it adds little or no wait. If Jev says you're not done ("until the period is over"), nothing is sent and it listens on; if Jev can't be asked, the send word sends. "end the call" ends it, once Jev confirms it's a goodbye. A send word in the middle of a sentence sends nothing. Whatever you said before a pause in the same turn goes with the send word, also when you say it on its own after the pause, and what you say right after it in the same breath ("Over. Build it.") is the start of your next turn. Everything the microphone hears goes to it, not only what counted as your speech; a reply's sound doesn't (if you talk over a reply, the half second before it stopped does). At a pause without a send phrase Jev still decides, from the live text. If it fails, or its text looks stuck (a long stretch of speech with hardly any words), your words are written down from their audio as below, so none are lost, and the screen says why. The connection closes after a minute without speech (the providers bill the time it's open) and opens again when you speak. Off, or without a key: as below.
- **Sending:** just talk, then pause. Nothing is written down while you talk; at each longer pause (1.8 s by default) what you've said since the last pause is written down, and Jev, the decision model, judges from all of this turn's words whether you're done: a question fully asked, a finished request, "over", "that's it". If you are, it's sent, written down once more in one pass, which gets the words right at the pauses too (it's started at the pause, alongside, so it rarely adds a wait); if you seem mid-sentence or have more to say, it listens on and asks again at the next pause. Writing down shows as **Writing down… N s**; if the speech model hangs for 6 s it's asked once more, and if that hangs too your words are kept for the next pause or Send. The screen says each time what it decided and why ("Sent: you asked your question (98%)", "Not sent: you seem mid-sentence (4% done)"). **Send now** (panel, screen, notification, also on the lock screen) and the headset or car media button always send at once, without asking; Send also writes down sounds that didn't count as speech just before. After a long silence (15 s by default) what you said goes anyway; if it was nothing, your turn just ends. If Jev can't be asked, "over" still sends.
- **Replies:** your chief first gives a real first answer, then, when the work is done, its final answer; both are read aloud. The chief hears when a call starts and ends, so it only speaks while you're on the call. The full answer stays in the chief's thread.
- **A reply never plays during your turn:** your turn is while you're speaking, while you have words not sent yet (also in a long pause to think), and while your message is written down and sent. A reply that arrives then waits; it plays as soon as nobody has the turn, for instance right after your message went. With live transcription your turn is the words it heard since your last message, so noise or a cough doesn't hold a reply back; without it, the parts of your speech not sent yet. A long silence (15 s by default) ends your turn: what you said is sent. A reply waits a minute at most without new words from live transcription, so a TV or a message bb won't take can't hold it forever; a silence send that fails is tried again after 15, 30 and then every 60 seconds. While a reply's voice is being fetched the screen counts the seconds. When the call ends with replies still waiting, the chief is told once, with the end of the call.
- **A talker:** in Settings → Hands-free you can name a second bb thread as the talker. Each hands-free message then goes to it and to your chief at the same time: the talker answers you by voice right away (often just "Checking."), while the chief does the work and sends its results to the talker, which tells you the answer. The app installs the talker's instructions as a bb skill, `dictate-and-delegate-talker`, next to the chief's two, and tells the talker once to use it (again when the skill changes).
- **With a talker, replies to an older message aren't played:** when you've sent a newer message, a reply to the one before no longer fits, so it's dropped (also an update that was waiting when you sent). Your next message, or the end of the call, tells the talker and the chief which replies you didn't hear (several as one note: how many, and the first one's start), so the talker can repeat what matters or ask you.
- **Talking over a reply:** you can talk at any time, also on the phone's speaker. A call plays like a phone call: replies are call audio, on a headset if one is connected (Bluetooth, wired or USB), else on the speaker, never the earpiece, and the volume keys change the call volume. The phone's echo cancellation takes the reply out of the microphone, so about 0.3 seconds of your speech stops it at once; what you say goes on as your turn, and the rest of the reply is dropped: it isn't played again, and your next message tells the chief how far you heard it (or the call's end does, if you send nothing more), so it repeats only what still matters. A clatter or a short loud sound doesn't stop it. Over Bluetooth, a call uses the headset's call mode, which sounds narrower than music.
- **Taking turns:** whoever starts first gets the turn. You can keep talking while the chief works: that's simply the next message.
- **Ending:** say "end the call" or say goodbye: Jev recognises it at the pause, and what you said before it is sent first. Or tap the red hang-up: whatever you said that wasn't sent yet is sent first, before the chief hears the call ended.
- **How it listens:** the phone notices when you speak and when you pause, without a model. To take the turn your voice has to be clearly louder than the background (3× by default) for 0.4 seconds within 1.5 seconds, so natural speech with its little gaps counts; a short sound ten times louder than the background, and loud like a voice close to the phone, counts too. Shorter sounds, distant voices and music mostly don't. Once the turn is yours, a short "over" counts. The background is learned again quickly once the noise at the start of a call has passed. Silence between stretches of speech never goes to the speech model (which otherwise invents "Thank you." for it), and the send and end words are given to it as hints.
- **Costs:** each message is kept as a note with its full cost: the transcriptions and the voice that read the replies to it. In your notes a call is one entry with its total cost; tap it to see its messages. Usage and Activity count the voice like the other models.
- **Hearing every word:** half a second of silence plays before each reply, so a phone or Bluetooth route that wakes up with the first sound doesn't swallow the first words; other sound gets quieter while a reply plays, and a reply is never cut while it's still playing.
- **Replies start with their first words:** a reply plays as soon as a fifth of a second of its audio has come, while the rest still comes in (Fish Audio sends a long reply over about four seconds). The call's audio is kept open, silent, for the whole call, so a reply's first sound comes out at once (Settings → Hands-free → Keep the call audio open; off, each reply opens its own and starts about half a second later).
- **A slow voice never keeps you waiting:** if the voice model hasn't started sending a reply's audio within 3 seconds (Kokoro sometimes takes 10 seconds or more; a second request goes out after 1 s), Android's voice reads that reply instead. On a phone without Android's voice (no text-to-speech engine, as on GrapheneOS) the voice model is waited for instead. A reply that still can't be played is tried once more before your next message tells the chief you didn't hear it.

Settings → **Hands-free** changes the send and end words (clear cues for Jev, and what decides when Jev can't be asked; several allowed, comma-separated), the pause after which it checks whether you're done and the silence before sending anyway, how much louder than the background you have to be and how much voice it takes to take the turn, the sounds, how long the voice model may take before Android's voice reads instead, whether the call audio is kept open, live transcription (on by default; it works once a key for its provider is saved) and its model: AssemblyAI Universal-3.6 Pro (most accurate, the default), ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime, or AssemblyAI Universal Streaming English (fastest and cheapest, less accurate), with your keys for them, and shows the recent events with their timings, the last thousand across calls, each call starting with a "── call started ──" line (Copy events for a bug report). The voice is a model like the others: Settings → Models & providers → **Text-to-speech**, each with its price: Kokoro (cheapest, the default), Fish Audio Drama 3 (Sarah, Friendly, Adrian, Ethan), Gemini Flash Lite TTS (Kore, Puck, Charon, Aoede), HeyGen Voice 1 Turbo (Bram, Nadine, Lucian, Tabitha), Voxtral Mini TTS (Paul, Paul cheerful, Jane, Oliver), MAI-Voice 2.1 Flash (Harper, Iris, Jasper, Ethan; the quickest, about half a second), ElevenLabs Flash v2.5, or Android's voice (offline, free; also the fallback when OpenRouter fails or is slow). When a voice model fails, the screen, the call bar and the log say why, and the next replies don't wait for it.

## Attachments

Talking about something, like a flat listing you're replying to? While you record, tap the 📎 on the floating panel (or **📎 Add** on Home) and **Paste what you copied** or **Attach a file** (images, PDFs, text, up to 10 MB). Attachments belong to that dictation: Refine gets them (PDFs are read as text by OpenRouter's file parser). Without Refine, they're simply kept with the note. They stay on the phone with the note for 7 days, like its audio.

## Templates

Settings → **Templates** keeps texts you write again and again, like an application for an apartment: a name and the text. Say which one you want and what's different this time, for example "use my apartment application for the two-room flat on Hauptstraße 5, the viewing is on Saturday, and mention that I work from home". Jev sees only your template names; only the template you ask for goes to the text model, and naming one starts Refine by itself. Templates are stored encrypted on the phone.

## Notes and costs

Open a note to copy it (**Copy**), edit it, or switch between **Dictate & Delegate's version** and **What you said**. Each note shows what it cost, as OpenRouter reported it for each call; when OpenRouter reports no cost, Dictate & Delegate shows none rather than $0.00. **Redo with a smarter model** transcribes the saved audio again with a more accurate speech model.

Settings → **Costs and activity** → **Activity** lists your latest notes with each step: the transcription (speech model), Jev with what it answered (e.g. "refine 0.82 · chief 0.97"), Refine, and the hand-over to bb, each with the time it took and what it cost (bb costs nothing here), or the error if a step failed. **Usage** shows each month's minutes and cost by model; Home shows this month and the last 14 days. Your OpenRouter account has the authoritative numbers.

## Models and keys

Settings → **Models & providers** has both keys and the model for each job: **Transcription** (the speech model), **Decisions** (Jev, with its two switches; there is no other decision model to pick) and **Refine**. **Refine** lists the text models in OpenRouter's catalog with their prices per million tokens, searchable; the default is Claude Sonnet 5.5.

Have Anthropic API credits? Settings → **Models & providers** → **Anthropic key** (optional) takes your own Anthropic key; Dictate & Delegate checks it with Anthropic and keeps it encrypted. Then ✨ Refine goes to Claude at Anthropic directly, and **Refine** offers the current Claude models (Sonnet 5.5 by default, Opus 5.5, Haiku 5.5, Fable 5.1) in an **Anthropic** tab; the **OpenRouter** tab picks the fallback. Speech and Jev stay on OpenRouter, so the OpenRouter key is still needed. If Anthropic fails (key, credits, an outage), the same request goes through OpenRouter, to the OpenRouter model, and the note says why when it was the key or the credits. Anthropic reports no price, so Dictate & Delegate computes it at the model's list price (Sonnet 5.5: $2 / $10 per million tokens) and marks it "≈". Because it comes out of credits you already have, it's kept apart: the note shows "Refine: Anthropic credits ≈…" on its own line, Usage shows "Anthropic credits used this month", and neither counts in the OpenRouter totals. A fallback through OpenRouter counts as real money.

## Data

Dictate & Delegate has no server, so everything it keeps is on the phone, in its private storage, which other apps can't read and which isn't backed up. Your keys, bb sign-in and ntfy topic are also encrypted with a key in Android's Keystore; notes, templates and the last transcript are not, so the app opens quickly however many notes you have. Settings → **Data on this phone**: **Keep audio for 7 days** (on by default) keeps recordings for redos, at most 100 MB, and **Delete saved audio** keeps text and costs. **Delete local data** removes notes, audio, templates, words, About you, both keys, your bb sign-in and settings from the phone; your accounts at OpenRouter, Anthropic and bb stay as they are. Dictate & Delegate's own language follows the system or Settings → **Using Dictate & Delegate** → **Language**.

**Network log:** to find out why a message didn't reach bb or a reply didn't come, the app keeps a line for every network request it makes: when it started (local time with milliseconds and the UTC offset, to match against bb's logs), what it was for, the request ID, the host and path, the HTTP status or the kind of failure (DNS failure, connect or read timeout, TLS error, connection reset, 502…), how long it took, the retry and the network at that moment (Wi-Fi or mobile, the carrier and whether it's the data SIM, IPv4/IPv6), plus the ntfy stream's connects, messages (by ID) and disconnects. Never keys, tokens, message text or audio. It keeps the last 1,000 entries of the last 7 days. **Copy network log** is in Settings → bb and under Hands-free → Recent events; **Copy last call** adds the entries since that call started. Delete local data removes it too.

**Updates:** about once a day the app asks GitHub whether there's a newer version (only that; no account, nothing about you). If there is, Home shows **Update to …** with what's new; tap **Update**: it downloads the APK, checks it (checksum, and that it's signed like your app) and hands it to Android, which asks you to confirm. The first time, Android asks you to allow installs from Dictate & Delegate. Settings → About → **Check for updates** checks at once.

Settings → **About** says it in one line: your voice and text go only to the providers you choose, and there's no Dictate & Delegate server. It also has the source code (MIT License) and **Report a problem** (GitHub issues).
